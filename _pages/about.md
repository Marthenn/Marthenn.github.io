## About
Hi! I'm **Marthen** (Bintang Dwi Marthen), a **PhD student in Computer Science** at [the Harvard John A. Paulson School of Engineering and Applied Sciences (SEAS)](https://www.seas.harvard.edu/), advised by [Prof. Juncheng Yang](https://junchengyang.com/).

I build systems that make **data-intensive** and **AI workloads** more efficient. My current interests include **caching and storage systems**, **learning-augmented systems**, **cloud and serverless computing**, and **systems for agentic AI and high-performance computing (HPC)**. I am most interested in simple mechanisms that are easy to deploy and that improve performance, robustness, and resource efficiency.

Currently, I study how coding agents use HPC clusters. In our [SC26 workshop paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38723), we found that users who run coding agents are only 19.5% of the users we observed, but they account for 55.8% of the job submissions and 42.7% of the GPU-hours. Since today's HPC interfaces and policies are designed for human-driven workflows, we argue for co-design: facilities should treat agents as first-class users, and agents should be aware of the facility they run on.

Before Harvard, I completed fast-track bachelor's and master's degrees in Computer Science at the [Bandung Institute of Technology (ITB)](https://www.itb.ac.id/) and graduated summa cum laude from the bachelor's program. In my [master's thesis](https://digilib.itb.ac.id/gdl/view_data/otomatisasi-manajemen-katalog-citra-dasar-kontainer-berbasis-telemetri-beban-kerja-untuk-mereduksi-latensi-cold-start-23525053), I used workload telemetry to automate the management of a container base-image catalog, with the goal of reducing cold-start latency in serverless platforms.
